history heteronym inline marker

history · internal reference · approved 2026-11-14

What happened

A speech engine picks one pronunciation for each spelling. In one of our renders the past tense of a common verb came out with the present-tense sound, and a speech-to-text check could not catch it because it writes the same letters for either sound. We added a text check that runs before a render, and a marker that lets a lyric sheet state the spoken form of one word in braces. A person listened to a render that used the marker and confirmed the words came out as intended.

Lesson

When one spelling has two sounds, say which one before the take: reword the line, or mark the spoken form. Do not rely on listening back by machine.

Evidence

  • A lyric sheet can write a word with its spoken form in braces; the linter, the gate and the renderer read the spoken form (internal reference)
  • The marker was first used in a render on 2026-11-14 and the owner confirmed by ear that the marked words came out as intended (internal reference)

history mcp schema overhead

history · internal reference · approved 2026-11-11

What happened

A small local model was asked to do a simple documentation update and failed again and again. Every request carried the tool descriptions of every connected helper service, and together they pushed each request past 100k tokens, even after compaction. The model's window was under 50k. The helpers were not broken; there were just too many of them in the room.

Lesson

Every tool you leave switched on is paid for on every single turn. Turn on only what the task needs, do the task, turn it off again.

Evidence

  • With the full set of MCP servers enabled, requests never dropped below about 102k tokens even after compaction (internal reference)
  • The local model's context slot was 49,152 tokens (internal reference)
  • The house rule since then is to enable only the servers a task needs (internal reference)

history pace follows mood

history · internal reference · approved 2026-11-14

What happened

We compared tracks by the same artist that editors had labelled driving with ones labelled calm. On the separated voice, the driving tracks were about ten percent faster in syllables per second, in 10 of 12 artists, and again in a second draw with no track in common. The rhyme measures did not follow mood at all.

Lesson

Pace carries mood. The same voice goes only about a tenth faster for a driving track than for a calm one; it does not need to double.

Evidence

  • Driving-labelled rap tracks are about 10% faster than the same artist's calm-labelled ones in 10 of 12 artists, in each of two draws with no track in common (internal reference)
  • Within an artist, the rhyme measures on published text do not follow mood (11 artists) (internal reference)
  • The pace measure reads 0.92 to 1.06 of a planted speed change (internal reference)

history rhyme line free measures

history · internal reference · approved 2026-11-14

What happened

We wanted to measure rhyme from audio. Measures that depend on where lines end failed, because only about half of the real line ends fall on a pause that can be heard. Measures counted on the stream of words in a short window held up: how dense the rhyme is, how often an ending matches on two or more vowels, and how near or far the partner sits. Share of end rhyme could not be recovered from audio.

Lesson

Rhyme is more than the last word of a line. Count vowels that answer each other anywhere nearby, and stack two or more at a time; that is what can be heard and measured.

Evidence

  • No phrase-cutting rule finds more than about half of the published line ends (internal reference)
  • On 28 gated hold-out tracks from unseen artists, heard against published text agrees at 0.74, 0.78, 0.72, 0.75 and 0.86 for the five line-free measures, with levels within 1% (internal reference)
  • The line-free measures do not reproduce written end-rhyme or internal-rhyme share (-0.16, -0.17) (internal reference)

history voice breath phrasing

history · internal reference · approved 2026-11-14

What happened

Our synthetic voice sounded choppy. Measured on the separated voice, its typical phrase lasted 0.67 seconds and its longest 1.22 seconds, both under what real rap recordings do. The voice was uncompressed and dipped inside every line, so each dip counted as a pause. Running lines together and evening out the level brought the typical phrase to about one second, inside the real range.

Lesson

A phrase needs room: about a second of unbroken voice, around two short pauses a bar, and a quarter of the time left empty. Carry the line on one breath instead of chopping every word.

Evidence

  • On 467 real hip hop tracks the median phrase is 1.09 s (5th to 95th percentile 0.80 to 1.85 s), with 2.22 pauses per bar and 0.25 of the time paused (internal reference)
  • Our render before the change had a median phrase of 0.67 s and a longest phrase of 1.22 s (internal reference)
  • After run-on lines and a 4:1 voice compressor the median phrase was 1.04 s and all nine breath measures sat inside the real range (internal reference)
  • The pause measure found 0.97 of 4793 planted pauses (internal reference)

history voice level in mix

history · internal reference · approved 2026-11-14

What happened

One beat level was used for every beat style. In one style the voice ended up 14 dB under the beat and could not be heard. Real recordings keep the voice a few decibels under the beat, and the beat rises where lines repeat; ours fell there instead. Setting the level per style and lifting the beat a little under repeated lines brought every style close to the real figure.

Lesson

The voice sits in the mix, not on top of it: a few decibels under the beat, set by a meter for each style, with the beat allowed to rise at the hook.

Evidence

  • On real tracks the voice sits 3.66 dB under the beat at the median (internal reference)
  • With one beat gain for all styles the voice was 14 dB under the beat in one style; with a gain per style all six read -3.52 to -3.73 dB (internal reference)
  • Where lines repeat, real tracks rise by 0.13 (drums), 0.23 (bass) and 1.33 (other) at the median; ours read -1.99, -1.09 and -0.90 before a 2.5 dB lift and -0.46, 0.68 and 1.28 after (internal reference)

history voice tone body

history · internal reference · approved 2026-11-14

What happened

We measured the tone of the voice in three parts: body in the low band, brightness, and air at the top. Brightness and air were inside what real recordings show. Body was far outside: the voice carried too much low end and competed with the kick. A low shelf cut on the voice alone brought body inside, and nothing else had to change.

Lesson

Check tone in parts before changing anything. Ours had one fault, too much low end in the voice, and one cut fixed it; the bright end was already fine.

Evidence

  • Body on real hip hop voices runs -8.30 to 2.76 dB (5th to 95th percentile, median -2.11); ours read 7.79 dB (internal reference)
  • Tilt (-13.61 dB), air (-21.93 dB) and flatness (0.04) on our render were inside the real ranges (internal reference)
  • After a 12 dB low shelf cut at 250 Hz body read 1.83 dB, measured on the clean voice only (internal reference)
  • Seven of eight planted tone checks pass; movement of tone over time fails and is not used (internal reference)

incident llm deep context corruption

incident · internal reference · approved 2026-11-11

What happened

A local coding agent started mangling exact strings (ssh flags, paths, address octets) once its conversation passed roughly 100k tokens. The first suspect was the quantised key-value cache. A test with full-precision cache reproduced the same garbling, so that suspect was cleared. The model's own reasoning trace showed it noticing the damage and spiralling while trying to copy the text exactly.

Lesson

When a long session starts to corrupt exact strings, the cause can be depth itself, not a setting you can flip. The only reliable lever found was to compact or restart before the session gets deep. Test the favourite theory properly, and be willing to cross it off.

Evidence

  • Corruption appeared at roughly 100k tokens of conversation depth (internal reference)
  • Full-precision cache reproduced the same garbling, ruling out cache quantisation (internal reference)
  • Compacting or restarting before roughly 80-100k tokens is the only known mitigation (internal reference)

incident squid acl shared egress denial

incident · internal reference · approved 2026-11-15

What happened

Image pulls on a logging host failed intermittently with a registry access-forbidden error. Every registry failed at once, the public ones and the private one alike. The egress proxy's access control was evaluated before its general allow rules and denied anonymous requests that had no explicit policy. The proxy later restarted cleanly, and pulls worked straight after a manual restart. The trigger that made the denials start was never found. Why tunnelled HTTPS requests were denied at all is also unexplained, since the helper's logic should allow them.

Lesson

When one error appears against every upstream at once, suspect the shared path, not each account. The tell here was the pattern, not the message. A restart that works is not a root cause, and the write-up should list what is still unknown beside the fix.

Evidence

  • Pulls failed with the same forbidden error across every registry at the same time (internal reference)
  • The access-control rule was evaluated before the general allow rules, so unmatched requests were denied (internal reference)
  • A manual proxy restart made pulls succeed; the trigger was not identified (internal reference)
