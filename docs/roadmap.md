## The operator's loop and the two environments (design decision 15)
Measure outside, numbers in: crate (chosen reference material) -> measure -> target brief -> write and make -> measure our own output -> compare to target. The measuring side holds audio, library and transcripts; MC SOC receives only validated feature tables. Planned pieces: provenance on tables, reference-crate records without names, a target-versus-achieved report, and sample-table fixtures so the loop runs with no music.

## Workbench for the production engineer (idea, agreed in principle 2026-11-12)
A small local environment, on the measuring side, where the engineer samples and assesses voices, tracks and songs: a local MCP server (stdio) over the engines' measuring tools, also usable from the command line. About six tools: `crate` (named selection with a reason; manifest with names stays local), `measure` (numbers for a track or rendered voice line), `compare` (gap to a target card or brief), `calibrate` (own track versus its true lyrics), `audition` (cut a clip to a local file and return the path; no audio into the model's context), `export` (feature table with provenance for MC SOC). Rules: **text-free by construction**, because an MCP result enters the model's context, so the canary contract runs against the tool layer too; read-only on the library; writes only to a tables folder and scratch; speech-server key from the environment at launch (not `sudo cat`); small tool set, enabled only for production sessions; lives with the engines, with the tool contract and its test here.

## Measuring image, speech server kept external (idea, 2026-11-12)
Container for the measuring side: audio in read-only, read-only root, one output volume gated by feature-table validation, egress limited to the speech endpoint (best form: `--network none` plus one forwarded Unix socket). CI uses a fake speech server with canary words and checks `docker diff`, `docker logs` and the output volume. The speech server's own logging is a recurring ops check (speak a canary, search Graylog and the journal), because no container can prove what a service keeps. Open: size, GPU variant for separation, model licences. See design decision 15.

## Listening trials, ABCD (idea, 2026-11-12)
A web server in the same image family (a separate serve-mode container, its own tests) for blinded A/B/C/D comparison of variants of crates, samples, songs and albums, to tune iteratively. A trial = variants with the parameters that made them (hook speed, key shift, generation steps); order shuffled per rater, mapping kept server-side and revealed after rating; every variant loudness-matched and start-aligned, with looping; ratings are pairwise picks, a keep/drop signal and an optional note, stored as feedback records (data, never instructions); measured numbers shown beside each variant so preferences can be tied back to measurable differences; named raters via the OIDC proxy; counts reported, no claims of significance at small n. Two modes. **Screening**: the ABCD view above. **Optometrist mode** (fine tuning): forced choice between two renders at a time ("one or two?"), one parameter at a time from coarse to fine (hook speed, then key shift, then generation steps), step size shrinking as answers narrow it (a staircase), "about the same" as a valid answer (a flat region means any setting inside it will do, so pick the cheapest), a repeated pair to catch inconsistent answers, counterbalanced first/second order, a cap on trials per sitting, and a recorded **prescription** (converged setting plus how sure it is). Answers are feedback records linked into a trial (parameters, order shown, answers, result). Slow engines (real singing takes minutes per render) pick the next point from a pre-rendered grid instead of rendering on demand. Rules: serves only our own renders and numbers, never transcripts, and never other artists' audio (reference audio stays on the measuring side); loopback or LAN behind the proxy; no telemetry; CI proves serve mode returns no text and measure mode is unaffected.

## Persona tuning as a choose-your-path adventure (idea, 2026-11-12)
The way a character or persona is tuned out of a crate: a branching, detective-gamebook style walk. Each page offers two doors that are the real clusters in the crate's measurements (for example slow and deadpan versus fast and melodic), so options are grounded in data, not invented. Each choice adds a trait, coarse to fine: pace, delivery, tempo and feel, rhyme band, mood, look. The ending is a character card: invented name, visual direction (the hand-made puppet look), the voice and beat cards behind it, and the path taken, recorded as ids and numbers. Reopening a path lets the engineer fine-tune inside the chosen region with the optometrist-style comparison (listening trials). Options are traits and our own sample renders, never reference audio or artist names, so the crate stays abstract (persona-card rules). A volunteer analyst can play it to build their own character: their choices are self-declared taste, so no listening history or account is needed (consent record still applies; see taste to character). Format only: no characters, names or text from any existing series.

Labels are optional; each side has a profile like a persona card; the relationship is a kind (contrast, nested, overlapping, ordered) with effect sizes, separability and overlap; negatives verified or assumed; confound indicators.** Not built: the crate bundle in `exchange.py` still has the simple count/groups/hash/purpose shape and must change to this before any real bundle exists. **Never pool crates:** no merging or averaging across crates; thresholds are crate-relative; compare standardised relationships, and combine only compatible crates by replication (crate count, sign agreement, weighted effect size, heterogeneity). Flaw to fix: `persona.py` cuts terciles over a whole table, i.e. a pool, so today's cards are pool-relative; rebuild per crate. Next: crate bundle change, a replication summary over compatible crates, a command to turn a persona card and crate into bundles, then a first real contribution.

## Differentiable atom analysis engine (idea from the owner, 2026-11-12)
Analysis by synthesis: time-frequency atoms (Gabor or chirplet) initialised by peak-picking on a multi-resolution STFT, rendered by overlap-add per time tile (tile-based, as in splat rasterisation), fitted by backpropagating a multi-resolution STFT loss to the atom parameters, with split-on-residual and prune-on-low-energy every N steps, optionally a residual noise model and a perceptual loss. Lives with the engines as another analyzer behind the same contract. **Guardrail: the atom set is a compressed copy of the audio and can resynthesise it, so atoms never cross the boundary**; they are ephemeral working files (treated like stems: git-ignored, deleted). Only aggregate scalars leave (onset rate and timing regularity for swing, tonal versus transient share, glide and vibrato extent, residual noise share, reconstruction quality as a confidence). The feature-table contract already refuses lists and vectors. It needs no transcription, so the text-leak problem does not arise for flow, pace, delivery or beat measures. Calibration has ground truth on our own generated material (known tempo and swing from the beat maker, known notes from the melody scores). Expected difficulties: local frequency gradients (coarse-to-fine across resolutions, log-frequency parameters, peak-picked init), magnitude-only loss ignoring phase (add a small waveform or complex-STFT term), split/prune drift (atom budget, residual and gradient criteria), harmonic voiced speech (grouped voice atoms later). Prior art to read before building: matching pursuit, sinusoidal modelling, DDSP, 3D Gaussian Splatting, and any audio splatting work. Lit review done (`docs/atom-analysis-literature.md`): no direct prior art found for rap or voice; known obstacle is uninformative frequency gradients under a pure multi-resolution STFT loss, so initialise from peak-picking and a pitch track and keep an f0 term; use chirplets (chirp rate captures glide and vibrato); apply the published autocorrelation swing and onset methods on the atom-derived onset envelope; hip hop swing is sixteenth-note and subtle (median about 1.2:1). **Baseline results (2026-11-12, `song/reference-stats/baselines.py`, untracked in the `song` repo; corrected twice after errors of mine, latest correction last).** Clean synthetic beats are too easy: the existing analyzer reads 20 of 20 tempos and swings correctly (swing error 0.006). **The earlier finding that all methods fail on finished spoken-rap songs is WITHDRAWN.** It came from stale ground truth: the "verified" tempos were taken from `run_all.sh`, but the existing song files had been rebuilt since (the songs date from 2026-11-09, `beat-noir.wav` was regenerated on 2026-11-10, and the notes say newer tracks were rendered at 132 BPM). Re-rendering four songs now at tempos we set (70, 78, 92, 110, under temporary names, since deleted) with the cached voice clips, every method (the existing analyzer, onset-envelope autocorrelation, beat-grid fit) read all four correctly within 2%. The old files read differently (about 100, 104, 88 or 66, 66) because they are different renders, not because the methods failed. Consequently: (1) the premise "vocal rhythm pulls the tempo estimate" is NOT demonstrated; (2) the bar I set for the atom engine (beating a demucs drums stem on tempo) has no evidence behind it, and the quick tests of band-limited, percussive-component and beat-spectrum tempo ran against the stale truth and say nothing; (3) ground truth for our own material should be made by rendering, not looked up. What is still unmeasured: swing on finished songs (render with known swing via the beat maker's `--swing` and test), real-media behaviour (reference tracks have no truth, so use consistency checks), and anything the atom engine could add beyond the baselines. The atom engine stays an idea until a task the baselines actually fail is found.

**Dereverb versus ffmpeg (2026-11-12, one 3.7 s clip of our own speech with synthetic reverb of known rt60; indicative only).** `dereverb.py` (late-reflection spectral subtraction: 1024-sample frames, hop 256, estimate taken 50 ms back, scaled by the energy decay for `rt60`, gain floor 0.12 or about -18 dB) has no `nr` or `nf`; its late-reverb estimate IS the noise it cancels, per frame and per bin. ffmpeg has no dereverb filter; `afftdn` removes a stationary floor and delays its output by a fixed 25 ms (compensate with `atrim=start=0.025,asetpts=PTS-STARTPTS`; an uncompensated first comparison was invalid). At rt60 0.7, distance to the clean clip (log-spectral, level-matched; lower is better): untouched input 9.94; `dereverb.py` 8.15 (7.88 at strength 1.5); `afftdn` at its best 8.50 (nr=6, nf=-20). The `afftdn` optimum sits at the top edge of the allowed noise-floor range (nf improves monotonically from -45 at 9.47 to -20 at 8.50), so no interior optimum was found; nr of 6 to 9 is best and nr of 12 or more damages the speech (11.3 at 18, 13.9 at 24). Deriving the `afftdn` settings from `dereverb.py`'s own estimate did not help: the noise level it implies (-33 dBFS) is not the best nf, and letting `afftdn` learn a noise profile from the segment `dereverb.py` marks as tail-dominated (`sn start/stop` via `asendcmd`) scored 9.7, barely better than doing nothing. Reading: one stationary profile cannot stand in for a per-frame, level-following estimate, so keep `dereverb.py`; the nearest ffmpeg settings are `afftdn=nr=6:nf=-20` (0.35 dB behind on the clean-clip distance).

**Lifting atoms into a third axis (owner's question, 2026-11-12).** Splatting-style: atoms live in a higher space of measured properties and are projected to the spectrogram. Candidate axes: scale (duration and bandwidth; already implied by multi-resolution, and the axis that addresses the failure above), chirp rate (glide and vibrato), modulation rate (syllable rhythm about 4-8 Hz; vibrato), stereo pan (testable on our stereo songs). Rules: only measured properties, never invented information; each added axis adds local minima and makes the atom set a richer copy of the audio, so add one only when it demonstrably improves a measure.

## Crate graph for track and character creation (idea, owner question, 2026-11-12; design decision 18)
Link the poles of crates (same members, declared complement, measured proximity inside one universe, same claim) so creation walks a graph of axes: pick a having pole, follow a complement edge to the next axis, fine-tune along each with the optometrist trials. Links connect and never merge. A min-hash sketch per side would estimate track overlap between crates (also needed to make the replication summary's independence assumption checkable).

## Preferences as positions against crates (idea, owner, 2026-11-12; design decision 19)
Because crates carry no labels or names, an engineer's or volunteer's preferences can be recorded as numbers against crates: a position from -1 to +1 on a crate's axis, with the number of choices and the source (adventure, trials, operator). A taste profile is a vector of positions across axes, with no names and no listening history, and character creation reads it. It is personal data: consent and subject hooks first, stored locally and kept out of the community exchange by default.

## Separation models (2026-11-12)
BS-RoFormer installed for vocal and stem separation as an upgrade from htdemucs. Setup: a new virtual environment `song/venv-roformer` (Python 3.13, torch 2.15 with CUDA 13.2, `audio-separator` 0.48.0; installed straight from PyPI, not through the devpi cache, a miss; a small follow-up dependency went through the cache) and one checkpoint, `model_bs_roformer_ep_317_sdr_12.9755` (610 MB), kept in the shared models root at `/rpool/llm-models/roformer` with `venv-roformer/models` a symlink to it, the same pattern as ACE-Step's checkpoints.

**Truth-based comparison (2026-11-12, five of our own songs).** Each song was rendered twice with `sing.py` (with its beat, and with a silent beat to get the voice alone), and both separators were scored with a scale-invariant SDR against the voice part and the rest of the mix (the voice part is estimated as a least-squares scale of the voice-only render, so the references are approximate). Vocals SI-SDR in dB, htdemucs against RoFormer: tag-team 21.8 / 24.6, ghost 8.1 / 25.1, loop 6.5 / 26.7, noir 21.1 / 25.0, tall-tale 24.3 / 26.3; mean 16.4 / 25.5. Instrumental: 16.3 / 19.2, -6.1 / 16.9, -14.3 / 16.3, 13.7 / 17.8, 18.5 / 20.7; mean 5.6 / 18.2. RoFormer is better on all five and far better on ghost and loop, where htdemucs largely fails (a guess, unverified: those songs use the vocoder-processed voice, which htdemucs, trained on real singing, treats as an instrument). Caveats: five songs; our voice is text-to-speech, not a rapper; the run overlapped other heavy jobs. Speed: htdemucs on the CPU took 17 to 24 s per song of about 100 s; RoFormer on the GPU took 86 to 116 s, about real time, so it is for chosen sets, not the whole 6,700-hour library (half-precision and compile options exist for the verified models and are untried). A paper on how separation reshapes rhythm-relevant properties (arXiv 2609.04224) is unread.

## First crates from real tags (2026-11-12; built locally, nothing exchanged)
Four crates were drafted from the library's own tags and measured with `reference-stats/analyze.py`, all in `reference-stats/crate-drafts/` (outside this repo; the manifests, tables and bundles hold names or guessable hashes, so they stay local; `exchange.py check` has not been run on the bundles). Method: sides from the **genre tag** of an artist (artists whose tags name both sides left out), one track per dedupe key, 40 to 400 s (the analyzer skips the first 20 s), no live or mix flag, at most 8 tracks per artist, durations matched in 30 s bins (recorded `matched_on: duration`; loudness not matched). Rule of thumb that held: measuring costs about 2 files per second with 10 workers, the music is only read.
- **Hip hop versus rock** (contrast, membership verified, confidence low): 901 and 895 tracks, 297 and 211 artists, no artist on both sides. Carriers at artist level: sub-bass level (group effect +0.59, hip hop higher), loudness range (-0.62), tonal steadiness (-0.49), also mid-band level, low band and loudness. **Does separation or dereverb help the rhyme pilot? (2026-11-12, 10 tracks; indicative only.)** Ten of the pilot tracks (the 5 with least heard and 5 well heard) were separated with htdemucs on the CPU (about 45 s a track; the official checkpoint was downloaded once, 80 MB, with the owner's OK) and also passed through `dereverb.py` (rt60 0.7, strength 1.0, defaults, untuned), then transcribed again. Syllables heard per second: the five well-heard tracks were unchanged (3.40 / 3.37 / 3.41 and so on, medians 3.59, 3.64, 3.62 for raw, separated, separated plus dereverb); the five poorly heard tracks stayed poor (medians 0.89, 0.61, 0.55; one fell from 0.89 to 0.12). So the low tracks are not failing because of the beat or the room. Likely causes (not checked, nobody listened): instrumental or sung passages, spoken word, or a language other than English (the model is English-only). Separation can also hurt. End-rhyme share on the well-heard tracks moved by up to 0.3 between conditions (0.16 to 0.47 on one track), so single-track rhyme numbers are noisy and only crate-level comparisons mean anything. Decision: no separation or dereverb for the rhyme measures; keep the syllables-per-second filter and scale on raw audio (cheap, 6 s a track). Tried and not worth it on the hardware: RoFormer on the CPU (about 50 minutes a track; the 12 GB GPU has 9.5 GB held by another process) and the Ryzen iGPU (2 compute units, not ROCm-supported, owner not in the render group).

**Published rhyme density added (2026-11-12; uncalibrated).** `rhyme_profile.py` now also returns `rhyme_density`: rhymed syllables over all syllables (Hirjee and Brown's definition), with a deliberately simple matcher instead of their learned model: a word is rhymed when a different word within 3 phrases either side, or in its own phrase, has the same sound from its last stressed vowel to the end, and it counts the syllables of that tail (unstressed words and repeated words never count, so a looped hook adds nothing). Tests count by hand (3 of 4 syllables; 4 of 6 for a two-syllable rhyme; window edge; repeated and unstressed words). On 60 of our own lyrics files (known text) it has a median of 0.15 and a range of 0.07 to 0.27, against 0.74 for the old `density`, which is not the published measure and keeps its name for continuity. Published rap values are about 0.12 to 0.3, so the scale is right, but perfect-rhyme matching probably undercounts slant rhyme, and the measure has not been calibrated against transcripts. The full hip hop decade run started before this change, so it does not contain it; a second pass would need the speech step again (the words are never stored). The change lives in `reference-stats/` (a different repository from this one).

**Vowel-chain density, the engine's own rule (2026-11-12; uncalibrated).** The engine's rhyme tools (`multi_rhyme.py`, `rhyme_find.py`, `rhyme_plan.py`) call a rhyme complex when 2 or 3 vowel sounds match across line ends with the consonants ignored, so the published-style `rhyme_density` (exact tail, consonants included) cannot see the complexity the engine aims for and must not be the complexity target. `rhyme_profile.py` now also returns `vowel_chain2_density` and `vowel_chain3_density`: the share of syllables inside a run of 2 (or 3) vowel sounds that repeats in another phrase within 3 phrases either side, or twice in one phrase without overlap, consonants ignored. A reduced AH0 never anchors a run and a run made of the same words does not count. Hand-counted tests include "coffee" and "bossy" (same vowels, different consonants: chain 4 of 6, perfect rhyme 0). On 120 of our own lyrics files (known text): `vowel_chain2_density` median 0.29 (0.14 to 0.48), `vowel_chain3_density` median 0.03 (0.00 to 0.18), against `rhyme_density` 0.15, so under the engine's rule our songs have about twice the rhyme coverage that exact-tail matching sees, and 3-vowel chains are rare (as `multi3_share`, median 0.00, already showed). Use for the target comparison: measure the reference crate and our renders with the same instrument (`rhyme_profile` on transcripts), never judge our output by the engine's own spelling-based lint (circular). The hip hop decade run in progress started before these measures and lacks them; a second pass is needed to add both.

**Our own songs against the hip hop reference (first target-versus-achieved pass, 2026-11-12; indicative).** The 73 finished songs (`song-*.mp3`, test variants left out) were measured with the same instrument as the reference crate (`analyze.py`, 20 to 140 s excerpt); 57 could be measured, 16 are under 40 s and cannot be (the same limit as the reference). Against the hip hop versus rock reference (901 and 895 tracks): for the features that replicated, our songs sit on the **hip hop side** of the midpoint between the two reference medians for loudness range (95% of songs; median 2.2 LU against hip hop 2.4 and rock 4.1), tonal steadiness (100%; 0.63 against 0.69 and 0.74, low within hip hop at the 14th percentile), mid band (86%) and sub-bass (68%; -7 dB against -6 and -11). The one large gap is **loudness: -15.5 LUFS against -9.5 for hip hop and -10.4 for rock** (3rd percentile; 0% of our songs on the loud side). That is a mastering level, not a creative one, and it is also what the earlier loudness-matched crate removes from the genre comparison, so matching it would not change which features carry. Tempo is slower than the reference (84 against 93 BPM, 15th percentile; the estimator has a prior near 95), and swing leans the other way (0.52, but swing did not separate the genres). The low band reads as a tie (both reference medians are 0 dB because it is the reference band), so no side can be read from it. Caveats: our voice is synthetic and our beats are rendered, so features built for other purposes (`steady` was designed to spot chiptune voices) say little about taste; these are medians against a reference whose artists vary a lot; and no persona or target card was used yet, only the whole reference side.

## Design for early (cheap now, costly to retrofit)
- **Subject and consent on anything about a person.** An optional `subject` on records and derived works, and a consent record (who agreed, scope, expiry, revoke). Verify should refuse any item with a subject but no live consent. Do this before the first taste-based character exists.
- **Retention and deletion by id.** Anything derived from a person must be removable by one id, and revoking consent must delete it.
- ~~Provenance and confidence on feature tables~~ built 2026-11-12.
- **One registry pattern for every adapter kind** (publishers have it; memory, review, sink and analyzer should match), so adding one is a file plus a line.
- **Calibration as a first-class tool.** "Heard versus truth on material we hold" is a reusable harness, not a one-off script.

### Rhyme measures, hip hop 2000s vs 2010s (full run, artist-level)

Run through the speech server on 912 hip hop tracks (two sides); 842 kept, 70 dropped for under 1.5 syllables heard per second. The kept tracks come from about 150
artists per side. Ten rhyme measures were tested, including the exact stressed-tail `rhyme_density` and the vowel-chain measures that follow the engine's own rule
(consonants ignored).

**Result: no carrier.** The bar is |artist-level effect| >= 0.2 and p <= 0.005 (0.05 over 10 features). The two nearest are `rhyme_density` (artist effect +0.18,
p 0.009) and `end_rhyme_share` (+0.15, p 0.024). A positive effect means the first side, the 2000s, is higher, so both lean toward the 2000s (an earlier version of this paragraph said the 2010s, which was the wrong direction). Both miss the bar, and the track-level effects are smaller (+0.10 and +0.07). The vowel-chain
densities are flat (artist effect +0.03 and -0.01). Multi-syllable and internal rhyme show nothing.

Reading: this sample does not show the decades differing on rhyme density. A weak hint that exact-tail rhyme was higher in the 2000s is not a finding. It would need new artists to confirm,
and the transcription (12 s windows, speech model) is uncalibrated against hand-marked lyrics. Not tried: other genre pairs, or a loudness-matched subset.

**Granularity (same data).** Only 10 to 28% of each rhyme measure's variation lies between artists; the rest is track to track within an artist (one-way ICC over the 82 artists with two or more tracks; transcription noise inflates the within part, so these are lower bounds on artist differences). An artist-level test therefore discards most of the signal for traits as fine as rhyme technique. A track-level difference with a cluster bootstrap (resampling whole artists, 1000 draws) gives 2010s minus 2000s of -0.16 SD for `rhyme_density` (95% interval -0.31 to +0.01) and -0.12 SD for `end_rhyme_share` (-0.28 to +0.05); no interval excludes zero. Confirmation on unseen artists is not possible from this library: only 24 (2000s) and 20 (2010s) unused hip hop artists carry a year tag. Next step if this matters: measure per verse or per window instead of per track (peaks and bursts, which is where deliberate complexity would show), and compare within artists that span both decades.

### Test-retest reliability of the rhyme measures (60 hip hop tracks)

Each track was transcribed a second time with the 12 s window grid shifted by 6 s, and the rhyme measures recomputed. The agreement between the two runs (one-way ICC) is how much of a measure's track-to-track variation is stable and how much is noise from where the windows fall. The speech model and the audio are the same, so this is an **upper bound** on reliability: it cannot see systematic errors, such as words the model drops in very fast rapping.

| Measure | Test-retest ICC |
|---|---|
| `vowel_chain2_density`, `vowel_chain3_density` | 0.86 |
| `density` | 0.85 |
| `internal_share` | 0.82 |
| `longest_chain_share` | 0.76 |
| `rhyme_density` | 0.75 |
| `multi_mean`, `multi2_share` | 0.69 |
| `multi3_share` | 0.62 |
| `end_rhyme_share` | 0.59 |
| `n_syllables` (control) | 0.99 |

The measures that responded most to a known dense rhymer (`end_rhyme_share`, `multi_mean`, `multi3_share`) are the least reliable: 30 to 40% of their track-to-track variation is window noise. Dividing the between-artist ICC found earlier (0.10 to 0.28) by these reliabilities puts the artist share of the *true* variation at roughly 25 to 35%. That is higher than first reported but still a minority: most of what distinguishes tracks is within an artist, which supports measuring below the artist. Averaging several window grids per track would raise the reliability of the noisy measures at the cost of more transcription.

## Instrument validation against planted truth (2026-11-12 and 13; pilots on 20 to 66 real tracks, numbers only)

**Why.** Running contrasts with measures nobody had checked risked building a detector for the artifacts of our own songs. The roles are now fixed: real recordings are the reference and the calibration set; our own songs are only ever the thing scored. Each instrument is first tested by planting a known change in real audio and checking the measure reports it. Everything here ran in `reference-stats/crate-drafts/` (outside this repo); stems and manipulated audio were deleted after each run. All of it is pilot scale, hip hop only, solo artists only.

"Response" below is the share of a planted change the measure reports (1.0 is all of it). "Rank agreement" is Spearman between the measure on a clean vocal stem and on the same vocal after it was mixed over another artist's backing and separated again, which mimics the real pipeline.

### Voice fingerprint and voice count
Speaker fingerprints (ECAPA, 1.6 s windows) on real solo vocals, alone, over their own backing, and over another artist's backing.

| Test (AUC against pairs of different artists) | First 10 artists | 23 held-out artists |
|---|---|---|
| Same voice, same track, full mix | 0.87 | 0.87 |
| Same voice, other track, full mix | 0.79 | 0.82 |
| Same backing, different voice | 0.52 | 0.52 |

The fingerprint follows the voice and ignores the backing, and separation adds nothing (stem 0.88 and 0.80 on the first set). A count rule (fingerprints smoothed over about 7 s, average linkage cut at cosine distance 0.70, groups holding at least 15% of the windows) was fitted on the first set, where it was exact 92 to 100% of the time. **On held-out artists it is exact 66 to 70% and within one voice 96 to 97%** (18 artists; 5 of the 23 were dropped because their two tracks were the same recording on two albums). Refitting on the held-out artists does not improve it (57 to 75% across folds), so this is the method's ceiling. Mean counts track the truth for one to three voices (about 1.1, 2.0, 2.8) and undercount four (about 3.3). Reading: usable for comparing groups, not as a per-track count. On 48 library tracks (no separation), tracks crediting several artists counted more voices than single-artist tracks (effect +0.53, p < 0.001; the result weakens with a looser cut, +0.15 at 0.80). An earlier fixed-k version of the measure reported about 2.9 voices for one true voice and is withdrawn.

### Vocal gate and sound tagger
A pretrained sound tagger (PANNs CNN14, 527 classes) against vocal-stem loudness as truth, 31 tracks: the larger of its rapping, singing and speech scores separates voiced from silent windows with AUC 0.98 (rapping alone 0.93, singing 0.91, speech 0.37). The threshold was chosen on the same data. Planted percussion hits (soundfont samples, 15 hits in 10 s of a 30 s excerpt, 20 tracks): at the level of the music the tagger separates planted from clean excerpts for cymbal (AUC 1.00), tambourine (0.99), wood block (0.91), cowbell (0.89) and snare (0.84), poorly for hi-hat (0.71), and not for clap or maraca (the samples were not recognised even alone). At 18 dB below the music every class is near chance (0.53 to 0.75), absolute scores are tiny, and there is no timing (scores near a hit are 1.09 times those between hits). Reading: fine for coarse, sustained content and for gating vocals; not an event detector. Event timing would need an onset detector on the drum stem; parked.

### Loudness
Integrated loudness (EBU R128 through ffmpeg) on 20 real mixes: gain changes of -6, -3, +3 and +6 dB come back exact on every track, resampling changes nothing, and re-encoding to mp3 lowers the reading by a constant 0.4 LU (cause not checked; it matters when comparing our mp3 releases with lossless references). Loudness range falls under compression on most tracks (median -1.35 LU) but not all: direction only.

### Flow measures (vocal stem, 20 tracks, 60 s each)
Planted changes: speed (0.8 to 1.25, pitch kept), pitch shift (2 semitones either way, speed kept), pitch movement scaled around the median (0.5 and 1.5, by resynthesis), and eight inserted 0.6 s silences.

| Measure | As built | Replacement | Replacement result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Syllable rate | loudness peaks: response 0.76, weaker when fast | syllables in the words the speech server hears, per active second | response 0.92 to 1.06; rank agreement 0.95 |
| Pitch spread | response 0.39 to 0.46; reads 13% higher when only the speed or the pitch changes | same statistic on a neural pitch tracker (RMVPE) | response 0.78 to 0.87; leak 3% or less; rank agreement 0.94 |
| Pitch span | response 0.45 to 0.53 | neural tracker | response 0.89 to 0.95; rank agreement 0.95 |
| Pitch level | shift read as -1.91 and +1.90; rank agreement 0.71 | neural tracker | -1.96 and +1.93; rank agreement 0.98 |
| Pauses | median phrase gap: response -0.41 (wrong direction) | pause share and pause time from the stem's loudness (18 dB below its 95th percentile, 0.25 s or longer) | planted 4.8 s read as 4.9 to 5.1 s, 7 or 8 of 8 counted; share unmoved by speed; rank agreement 0.93 |

The heard rate and the acoustic rate rank the unmodified tracks differently (0.51), and neither has been checked against a true syllable count; the heard rate needs intelligible English and dropped 2 of 20 excerpts. Pause time over-responds to speed (about 1.3), so pause share is the safer number. The replacements are now in the measuring tool (`flow_profile.py`, engine side) as new columns beside the old ones, which are kept so earlier tables and voice cards stay comparable; the `flow-tsv` analyzer reads them (version 2; a table without them is still version 1). Folded-in spot check on one stem: speed 1.25 read as 1.245, a 2 semitone shift as +1.91, spread unchanged. Voice cards still use the old pace and delivery measures.

**Voice grouping against planted voices (2026-11-13, local test; splices exist in memory only).** Stretches of one reference stem were spliced into 180 s of another at known times, and the grouping was scored on what it could not know: how many voices it counts, and whose seconds end up in each voice's pieces. - **Turns of 30 s are found.** At a 30% share the guest gets its own voice in 14 of 15 composites, at 15% in 10 of 15. The pieces given to that voice are 91% to 94% the guest's (median; worst 75%), and they hold 75% to 79% of the guest's time. Where found, the main voice's pieces carry 1% to 3% guest time (median).

Otherwise it stays inside the main voice: a median 28% and 17% of the main voice's material is the guest, about what was planted. The rule smooths over 9 windows (about 8 s), which is the length of the turn.
- **Shorter smoothing trades one error for the other.** Re-run on the same fingerprints with 5 windows, 10 s turns are found in 13 and 10 of 15, but 2 of 12 untouched controls and 3 of 13 same-artist controls split into two voices. With 3 windows: 14 and 12 of 15, with 2 and 1 false splits. With none, half the untouched controls split. The built rule stays at 9; no setting was chosen on this test.

A track where voices trade short lines can read as one voice whose numbers are a blend, the same blend a track row would give. - **Limits of the test.** The hosts were picked by the rule under test, so the clean controls are partly circular. Guests are other artists from one reference set, close in genre. Splices are hard cuts between stems with different recording chains, which is easier than two voices in one session. One performer switching between rapping and singing was not tested. Overlapping voices were not tested.

**Level of pitch spread, against a pitch line that is known exactly (2026-11-13, local check).** A voice has no written pitch, so the truth is made: each 60 s cut of a vocal stem is taken apart with the WORLD vocoder and put back with a pitch line we choose, which is then the true pitch of the audio by construction, with the cut's own timbre, timing and voicing. ### Phrase cutting tried against published line ends (2026-11-13)
The same 26 tracks; twelve rules for cutting heard words into phrases, each scored on how many published line ends it finds and on how well seven rhyme measures then rank the tracks like the published text does. Pilot numbers, five artists.

- **No rule recovers written lines.** Pauses of 0.15, 0.25 and 0.35 s, local-maximum gaps, the largest gaps for a target line length, and a 2, 4 or 8 beat grid from the backing stem all find at most about half of the line ends exactly (best F1 0.50). The 4-beat grid lands within one word more often (0.63) but on the wrong word.
- **A shorter pause is slightly better.** Mean rank agreement of the seven measures: 0.15 s pause 0.54 against 0.52 for 0.25 s on all tracks, 0.69 against 0.64 on the 21 well-heard tracks, 0.57 against 0.46 with the largest artist left out. Rhyme density, which needs no line ends, agrees at 0.82 to 0.92 on well-heard tracks under every rule. The next step is measures that do not depend on line ends, not more cutting rules.

**Planted change in rhyme structure (2026-11-13, local test; spliced audio deleted as heard).** Published line times let a real vocal stem be cut into its lines and rejoined in another order: the words stay, the rhyme structure moves, and the true value of every measure is known from the published lines in the new order. 29 tracks with line times, four versions each: same order (what cutting alone costs), blocks of four lines shuffled, all lines shuffled, lines sorted by the vowel of their last word. Each version was heard again and its change from the untouched stem compared with the true change.

- **Cutting costs nothing.** Rejoined in the same order, every measure moves by under 0.005 on average, with track-to-track noise of 0.008 to 0.03 (the count of heard words is unchanged).
- **The measures move the right way, by about half.** Heard change over true change: rhyme density 0.36, assonance 0.53, near share 0.33, far share 0.55, multisyllable mean 0.63, share with 2 or more 0.61, with 3 or more 0.54, 2-vowel chains 0.73, 3-vowel chains 0.51, longest chain 0.23. Where the true change is larger than twice the cutting noise, the sign is right in 68 to 85% of cases. So a difference in rhyme between two tracks is real in direction and understated in size, by roughly half.
- **The loss is in the heard words, not the splicing.** Re-ordering the heard words of the untouched stem as text, with no new audio, gives the same slopes (0.36 to 0.59). The loss does not shrink on the best-heard third of tracks (0.49 pooled, against 0.48 for the middle third), so word errors alone do not explain it; line times that do not sit exactly on the sung words, and sung words outside the published text, would do the same and cannot be told apart here. The figures are therefore a lower bound on what the measures report of a real change.
- **Reading with the agreement above.** Levels match the published text and tracks are ranked alike (0.67 to 0.88), yet a change inside a track is half seen. Both hold when wrong words add about as much chance rhyme as they remove real rhyme: the level is kept and the contrast is flattened. Group contrasts on these measures will be understated, not invented.
- **Limits.** The plants are small: shuffling lines moves a measure by about one between-track standard deviation or less, two to three times the cutting noise, so single-track slopes are noisy (correlation of heard with true change 0.1 to 0.5). Only line order was changed, which barely moves near share; changes inside the line are in the paragraph after the next. Longest chain is the weakest (0.23, noise near the size of the plant) and should not go on a card.

**Built in: four measures and a gate that needs no lyrics (2026-11-13, local tool `rhyme_profile.py`, outside this repository; 34 local tests pass).**

- **The four.** Rhyme density, share of stressed words whose ending matches on 2 or more vowels, 2-vowel chain density and far share, as `stream_*` beside the older line measures, given whenever a track has 40 syllables. Together they cover perfect rhyme, multisyllable matching, vowel chains and line-to-line spacing with the least overlap; longest chain is dropped. Each has hand-counted tests, and the port gives the same value as the draft on all 63 cached word lists.
- **The gate.** A new track has no published text to say how much of it was heard. The tool now hears a track twice, the second time with its 12 s windows shifted by 6 s, and takes the share of words the two hearings agree on in order. Where the recogniser is guessing, the hearings disagree. Under 0.70 the track keeps its size measures and the agreement figure, and its rhyme measures are withheld, not estimated.
- **How the gate was chosen.** Tried on the 34 tracks whose heard share is known from published lyrics. Rank agreement with the heard share, and how well each separates tracks under 60% heard from the rest (1.0 perfect, 0.5 chance): two-hearing agreement 0.79 and 0.82; the recogniser's own median word confidence 0.72 and 0.71; out-of-dictionary share -0.56 and 0.63; word rate, voiced share and word length carry nothing.
- **What it buys.** At 0.70 the gate keeps 25 of 34 tracks. On those, heard against published: rhyme density 0.92, 2-or-more share 0.86, chain density 0.90, far share 0.65, levels within 6%. Gating on the true heard share, which needs the lyrics, gives 0.88, 0.79, 0.81 and 0.68 on 26 tracks. Thresholds from 0.60 to 0.75 give the same picture.
- **Limits.** The threshold was set and scored on the same 34 tracks; leave-one-out calls 28 of 34 right against the 60% line. The gate withholds 5 of the 8 poorly heard tracks and lets 3 through, and withholds 4 of the 26 well heard. One speech model, rap only, and it doubles the hearing time. The planted-change result above still applies: a change inside a track is about half seen. Run on our synthetic voices in the next paragraph.

**Rhyme changes planted inside the line (2026-11-13, local check).** The earlier plant moved whole lines; here each published line of six or more words is cut in two near its middle and the halves are moved, so rhymes change place within and between neighbouring lines while every word stays. A cut inside a line has no published time, so it is taken from the first hearing: just before a heard word that matches the published word at that place, with its neighbour matching too. 29 tracks with line times, 26 usable (3 had too few lines that could be cut); a median of 57% of lines were cut (lowest 28%). Conditions: cut and rejoined in place; the two halves of each line swapped; neighbouring lines woven (A1 A2 B1 B2 to A1 B1 A2 B2); all halves shuffled; halves sorted by the vowel of their last word. Truth is the measure on the published words in the new order. Spliced audio was scratch and deleted as heard.

- **The extra cuts cost nothing.** Cut and rejoined in place, the heard measures move by 0.001 on average with a spread of 0.008 to 0.013, the same as for cuts at line ends.
- **Swapping and weaving are not a test.** They move the true measures by 0.003 or less on average: within a 24-syllable reach, a rhyme moved by half a line is still in reach. Only shuffling and sorting the halves move anything (0.02 to 0.04, as large as the whole-line plants or larger).
- **Heard change is about half the true change, with the sign mostly right for three measures.** Heard on true over the four planted conditions, slope, correlation, and share with the right sign where the true change is clear: far share 0.53, 0.60, 78% of 50; 2-or-more share 0.50, 0.52, 77% of 44; near share 0.47, 0.51, 71% of 42; chain density 0.28, 0.29, 66% of 38; rhyme density 0.37, 0.28, 55% of 31. Restricting to tracks with 60% of words heard changes nothing.
- **Two measures fail inside the line.** Rhyme density gets the sign right no more often than chance. Chain density follows the shuffle (slope 0.51) but misses the sort entirely: chains truly rise by 0.031 and the heard value does not move (slope 0.10), so chains built by bringing like line-halves together are not heard as chains.
- **What it means.** Far share, 2-or-more share and near share respond to changes inside the line at about half size, as they did to changes of line order; they can rank a large rearrangement, not measure a small one. Rhyme density and chain density should not be read as showing where in the line the rhymes sit. Near share, not built in at the time of this test, did as well as the built-in ones here; it was built in the same day (next paragraph).
- **Limits.** The cut places come from the recogniser under test, so only well-heard places in well-heard lines are cut, which flatters the result. Halves cut mid-phrase and reordered are not natural speech. One hearing per condition, one speech model, 26 tracks, and the planted changes are still small against the between-track spread.

### Interludes and what the accompaniment does before a voice enters (declared 2026-11-13, not run)
**Why.** Writing a list-song for the Robot Mafia (a fast spoken list, sung hooks between the verses) raised two questions the repo cannot answer: how long are the instrumental gaps between vocal sections in the reference crate, and what are the instruments doing in the bars just before a voice comes back in (a drop-out, a drum fill, a riser, or nothing)? Today the only stored numbers are pauses inside the vocal (reference table, 50 stems: a median of about 10 pauses of 0.25 s or more a minute, 4.8 to 27.5 between the 10th and 90th percentile; a median of 18.5% of the stem inside pauses). Nothing is stored on beat-only stretches, and the bar-by-bar tool (`intro_survey.py`) has no saved results.

**Declared before the first run (rules fixed first; changing one afterwards is reported as a change).**
- **Sample:** 12 rap tracks and 12 pop/rock tracks from the library, 120 to 360 s, at most 2 tracks per artist, so each side has at least 8 artists. Chosen by a script from tags and duration before any audio is read. The two sides are reported separately and never pooled. Counts of tracks and artists are published; names are not. **Changed before any run (owner, 2026-11-13):** the rap side is instead every already-separated hip hop track of 120 to 360 s (from 516 tracks of 109 artists, at most 8 per artist, chosen earlier by tags, lyrics and a fixed seed for the line-level work, before any audio was read); the count after the duration rule is published with the result. The pop/rock side stays at 12, so the two sides differ in size and the pop/rock side stays the weaker one. The reading rule is by artist, so the larger per-artist cap does not let one artist carry a pattern.
- **Separation:** each track is separated into vocals, drums, bass and other (htdemucs). The stems are scratch audio: kept in a local cache outside every repository and reused between runs until the owner says the testing is done (owner, 2026-11-13; changed before any run from "deleted after the run", to save separating the same tracks again), then deleted. Nothing but numbers leaves the measuring side.
- **Tempo and bars:** tempo from the drums stem; kept only where it agrees with the existing analyzer's tempo within 3%. Tracks where the two disagree are dropped and counted, not corrected. A bar is four beats.
- **A vocal entry:** the first frame of voiced activity after a vocal gap of at least one bar (the same activity rule as `flow_profile.py`: speech-band level within 35 dB of the vocal's 95th percentile). Gaps shorter than a bar are the existing pause measure and are not counted here.
- **Gap length:** the instrumental gap before each entry, in bars (rounded to the nearest half bar). Reported as a median and the interquartile range per side, and the share of entries after a gap of 1, 2, 4 or 8 bars or more.
- **Before the entry:** for each entry and each stem (drums, bass, other), the level over the two bars before the entry divided by its level over the eight bars before that (a ratio, 1 = no change), the drum onset density ratio, and the 5 to 10 kHz energy ratio of the other stem. Class, by a rule written now: **drop-out** if any stem's ratio is under 0.3 in the last bar; **fill** if the drum onset density ratio is above 1.5 in the last half bar; **riser** if the 5 to 10 kHz energy of the other stem climbs across both bars to above 1.5 times its baseline; **hold** if all ratios stay between 0.7 and 1.4; otherwise **mixed**. A class is checked in this order, and an entry takes the first it meets.
- **Reading:** this is a description to set the song's design numbers, not a hypothesis test. A pattern is called typical for a side only if at least 8 artists contribute and it is the most common class in at least 70% of those artists. Otherwise the side is reported as having no typical pattern.
- **Our own songs afterwards:** rendered twice (with and without the beat, as in the separation truth check), so the vocal and the accompaniment are known without separating, and measured by the same rules, as the first target-versus-achieved look at structure. Not before the reference result is written.

**Not done.** Nothing has been measured. The accompaniment is a mixed bag by genre and decade (rap beats are often loops that drop out; rock changes by arrangement); the stem separation of an accompaniment is not validated against planted changes here (only the voice separation was); a drop in the drums stem can be a separation artefact. If a validation is wanted first, plant known drop-outs and fills in our own rendered beats and check the classes are recovered, as for the other instruments.

**The other direction: the accompaniment should know where the voice is (owner, 2026-11-13; declared with the analysis above, nothing run).** Today the beat is made first and only ducked under the voice (`sing.py`), with fixed fills at bars 8 and 16 (`make_beat.py --fills`) and a fixed intro, so a drop-out or a fill can land on top of a word. The analysis above gains two more windows, declared now with the same rules (the same sample, the stem separation, the activity rule, the 8-artist and 70% reading): **during** a vocal section, the accompaniment level and its onset density per bar against how dense the vocal is in that bar (words, or syllables where known; low, middle and high thirds of density), reported as a ratio to the same stem in the gaps of that track; and **after** a voice leaves, the same ratios for the two bars after the last vocal frame before a gap of at least one bar. Together the three windows say how real accompaniments leave room (what thins out under a dense line, what rushes in to fill a gap, what announces an entry).
- **A numbers-only vocal map**, built for our own renders (`song/vocal_map.py`, prototype, tried on one render): from the line timing `sing.py` already writes it takes, per bar, the share of the bar with a voice and the number of words begun, plus the entries (a line after a gap of a bar or more, with the gap in half-bars) and the exits. It reads the text only to count words and keeps none; the output is checked to hold no string, so it can cross the boundary like a feature table. A generator would read it to place a drop-out, fill or riser before an entry, thin out under a dense bar, and leave a gap alone.
- **Order of building:** the reference analysis first (it sets the numbers), then a `--vocal-map` option on the beat maker that applies them, then the same analysis on our own renders as the target-versus-achieved check. Voice first, bed second may be needed (render the voice alone, make the map, then make the beat to fit it), which reverses today's order and is a change to the engine side, not this repository.
- **In this repository:** the Act seam can carry it without changing its rules: a target can list the structure bands once measured (interlude lengths, class shares before an entry), and the engine returns numbers only. Nothing is added yet because the bands do not exist.
- **Not done:** all of it. The prototype map's tempo is an input (the demo used a guessed 92 BPM, so its bar numbers are illustrative); the beat maker does not read a map; and the plant test (known drop-outs and fills in our own rendered beats, recovered by the classes) has not been built.

**What the line-level runs already settle for this plan (2026-11-13, outside this repo; numbers only, added before any run of the above).** A sibling piece of work read the same four stems against typed line start times instead of bars. It changes four things here.
- **Part of the validation exists, on real material.** 28 mixes of one real voice over another artist's backing, with known changes planted, separated again and read back: drum cuts at line starts found 0.91 with 0.035 false finds; drum fills ranked 0.89 against the truth; a level lift on chosen lines read within 0.2 to 0.4 dB on all three stems. What that plant did not cover: bar positions, the riser rule, onset density under a voice, and the classes as written above.
- **The "during" window is biased as declared.** A stem's level under the voice against its level in the gaps passed only for "other" (error 0.85 dB). The drums read 2.3 dB low wherever the voice is, and the bass fails narrowly: separation takes level out of them under a voice. So a "thins out under a dense line" result for drums or bass is context only until that bias is planted and corrected; "other" can be read.
- **Line-level is already described; section-level is the open part.** On 81 mixed-genre tracks (29 artists; provisional): drum cuts at line starts are at chance (median 0.08 against 0.09 at mid-line), fills in line tails are at chance (1.01), the "other" stem is 2.1 dB up on repeated lines, and the stems enter staggered before the first line (medians: other 8.1 s, drums 4.7 s, bass 2.7 s). The entries after a gap of a bar or more, declared above, are therefore where a pattern can still be, not the gaps between lines.
- **The rap side needs no new separation.** Four stems of 516 hip hop tracks (109 artists) and of the 81 mixed tracks are in the cache (run in progress when this was written). The rap side is now drawn from them (the change is recorded in the sample rule above). The pop/rock side still needs its 12 separated.
- **Two rules above need a unit before the first run:** whether a level "ratio" (0.3, 0.7, 1.4) is of amplitude or of power (0.3 is 10.5 dB down in one and 5.2 dB in the other); and the tempo rule filters on tempo only, so bars here are lengths counted back from an entry, not downbeat positions.

**First run of the rap side (2026-11-14, outside this repo; numbers only).** Rules were fixed in the script before the run; ratios are amplitude, a bar is four beats.
- **Plant check (24 mixes of one real voice over another track's stems, 31 known entries): the entry finder fails.** After separation it found 0.68 of the known entries (rule 0.80) and 0.49 of what it found was extra (rule 0.20). Tempo passed (24 of 24 within 3%). The classes got no verdict: fewer than 20 known entries each (drop-out 13, with recall and precision 1.00; fill 5; riser 4; hold 2; mixed 7).
- **Real read, context only for that reason:** 471 hip hop tracks of 106 artists (516 cached, 482 of 120 to 360 s, 471 with the two tempo reads agreeing). 715 entries after a gap, a median of 1 per track; gap median 2 bars (4 or more bars: 0.22); intro median 4 bars. Class share: drop-out 0.28, fill 0.10, riser 0.07, hold 0.18, mixed 0.38. By the typical rule: no typical pattern.
- **Cause, and what is next:** the declared activity rule (35 dB under the voice stem's 95th percentile) calls the voice active in most frames, so separation bleed fills the gaps. Not tuned after seeing this. A second version is to be declared before any run: a stricter activity rule chosen on plant material only, entries cross-checked with typed line times, and enough mixes for 20 or more known entries per class. The pop/rock side waits for that.
- **Second version, same day (rule chosen on 16 separate mixes, then 60 new mixes with 604 known entries):** voice active = within 15 dB of the stem's 95th percentile, runs under 0.5 s removed. The entry finder still fails (found 0.75; extra 0.17, inside its rule). Passing: tempo (59 of 60), the drop-out reader (recall 0.95, precision 0.96) and the fill reader (0.88, 0.92), each at a known entry time. Failing: riser (recall 0.17), hold and mixed (precision 0.50 and 0.51).
- **What the second real read showed (context only):** the stricter rule calls the voice active in 0.13 of frames, and its entries sit at a typed line start no more often than a random moment (0.38 against 0.35). A level threshold on a separated voice does not find entries; that approach is dropped. Kept: tempo, and the drop-out and fill readers. Next, to be declared first: entry times from typed line times (a line start after a stretch without one), checked on a plant where line times are known. Our own renders carry exact line times and need no entry finder.

A break in typed line starts is mostly not a silent voice.

### Breath: pauses and unbroken runs of the voice (2026-11-14, local check outside this repo, pass rules fixed first; numbers only)
Rap is delivered in runs between breaths; a voice that stops inside every line, or never stops, does not sound like one. This measures where the voice is silent and how long it runs between silences, on real material first, then on our own render.
- **The measure.** On the separated vocal stem (300 to 3000 Hz level): a pause is a stretch more than 10 dB under the stem's 95th percentile lasting 0.15 to 2.0 s; a phrase is the voiced stretch between two pauses, with quiet runs of 0.5 s or more closing it. The two numbers (10 dB, 0.15 s) were chosen on 16 development mixes and not changed after. It measures silence, not breath: an audible breath and a silent gap read the same.
- **Plant check: passes.** 30 new mixes of one real voice over another artist's backing, 4793 pauses known from the clean voice before mixing, read again after a second separation. Pauses found 0.97, extra 0.09. Per track, rank agreement and ratio to the truth: pauses per bar 0.98 and 1.04; median phrase 0.97 and 0.99; 90th percentile phrase 0.95 and 1.00; median pause 0.86 and 1.00. Our own render, clean voice against separated mix: found 0.91, extra 0.09. Whether pauses sit in typed line gaps got no verdict (7 usable mixes).
- **Real ranges (467 hip hop tracks, 106 artists; 5th, median, 95th percentile over tracks; each holds in both halves when split by artist).** Pauses per bar 0.90, 2.22, 3.63. Median phrase 0.80, 1.09, 1.85 s (in beats 1.09, 1.67, 3.26). 90th percentile phrase 1.56, 2.50, 5.77 s (in beats 2.26, 3.77, 9.65). Longest phrase 2.31, 5.15, 13.83 s. Median pause 0.19, 0.21, 0.30 s. Share of time paused 0.11, 0.25, 0.39. Share of pauses at a typed line start 0.21, 0.37, 0.58, against 0.35 for a random moment: pauses are not tied to line starts.
- **Ours, before: too choppy.** One sheet, one voice pair, the default render: median phrase 0.67 s, 90th percentile 1.10 s, longest 1.22 s, all under the real 5th percentile; share of time paused 0.38, at the upper edge. The expectation written down beforehand was the opposite (runs too long); it was wrong and is recorded as such.
- **Cause.** Not pauses between lines. The synthetic voice is uncompressed and its level falls 10 dB or more between syllables inside a line, which the measure reads as a stop; real vocals are compressed and do not. Removing the pause at commas alone barely moved it (longest 1.22 to 1.71 s).
- **Change on the engine side, and the result.** Two renderer options: commas inside a line are not paused on, and a 4:1 compressor on the voice; the beat was then re-levelled so the instrument measures stay inside. Same sheet after: median phrase 1.04 s, 90th percentile 2.33 s, longest 5.49 s, pauses per bar 1.59, share paused 0.19; all nine breath measures inside the real ranges, voice minus beat -3.58 dB against a real median of -3.66, no instrument measure outside, the track gate passes with 0 of 11 outside. The owner listened and made it the engine's default.
- **For contrast, the first releases' recipe on the same sheet:** voice 11.5 dB over the beat, phrases 0.67, 1.10 and 1.22 s, three instrument measures outside. The track gate passes it too: the gate does not see level balance or run length.
- **Limits.** The measure rewards compression as such, so a compressed voice passes whatever it says. One sheet and one voice pair; the other styles and other sheets were tried afterwards (next points). Hip hop only. No breath mark was added to the lyric sheet format, because pauses were not the fault. Not built in this repository: the real ranges are not yet targets in a voice card, and the gate does not count them.
- **Other styles, same sheet (same day, context, no pass rule).** Run lengths do not change with the beat (they are read on the clean voice). With one beat gain for all styles the voice sat 3.58 dB under the beat in the style it was tuned on, 0.5 to 1.6 dB under in three others and 14.16 dB under in the trap style, outside the real range (-10.12 to 3.26). The engine now sets the beat gain per style; after that all six styles read -3.52 to -3.73 dB against the real median of -3.66. The owner listened and kept it. The track gate passed every render before and after: it does not see level.
- **Drum hits in line tails.** Two styles read just over the real range for drum activity in the last quarter of a line (1.19 and 1.26 against 0.86 to 1.18). In the trap style a hat roll at every bar end was the cause; moved to every fourth bar it reads 1.18, at the edge. Stopped there, since more would be fitting one sheet; the other style (0.01 over) was left alone. Not yet heard by the owner.
- **Fair test on sheets the tuning never saw: fails as declared.** Rules fixed before the first render: six of our other sheets drawn by seed, one per style, tempos 88 to 112, engine defaults only; a render is good if the track gate passes, all nine breath measures are inside and voice minus beat is inside; pass at five or more good of six and a median distance from the real median of 2.0 dB or less. Result: 2 of 6 good, median distance 0.76 dB. By part, read after the fact and so context only: voice minus beat inside in 6 of 6 (-5.08 to -2.17 dB), breath all inside in 5 of 6, the gate failed in 4 of 6, each time on measures of the lyric sheet (14-syllable lines, exact end-rhyme share over 0.70, few multi-syllable endings: sheets written under the older line rule). The sixth is a short spoken sheet that fails both. The rule mixed the sheet with the render and could not tell them apart; that is a fault in the test's design. It was not rerun under other rules. What it shows plainly: most of our older sheets do not meet the current lyric rules.

### Voice tone: brightness, air, body (2026-11-14, local check outside this repo, pass rules fixed first; numbers only)

Part A of the reference design had no instrument for the tone of the voice. This one reads band levels on the vocal stem; it does not say whether a voice sounds human.

- **Measures**, over the frames where the voice sounds (within 10 dB of the stem's loud level), as band levels against 300 to 1000 Hz: tilt (2 to 5 kHz), air (5 to 10 kHz), body (100 to 300 Hz); also how much the tilt moves from frame to frame, and spectral flatness in 300 to 3000 Hz.
- **Plant check (30 mixes; 8 verdicts, 7 pass).** A real vocal stem over another artist's backing, separated again, once untouched and once brightened by a known shelf (0 dB at 1 kHz rising to +6 dB at 4 kHz). Level rule per measure: rank agreement before and after separation 0.80 or more and a median shift within 1.5 dB. Tilt, air, body and flatness pass (0.97, 0.98, 0.98, 0.83; shifts -0.27, -0.49, -0.25 dB; flatness reads 0.83 of its value). Tone movement fails (0.69) and is context only. Change rule: the planted brightening is read at 1.03 (tilt) and 1.04 (air) of its size, and body moves 0.13 dB against a limit of 0.5.
- **Real ranges (466 hip hop tracks, 106 artists; 5th, median, 95th):** tilt -20.97, -14.79, -8.88 dB; air -32.45, -25.62, -19.34 dB; body -8.30, -2.11, 2.76 dB; flatness 0.01, 0.03, 0.07. All four hold when the artists are split in two halves.
- **Ours (default render, voice separated from our own mix, like for like):** tilt -13.61, air -21.93 and flatness 0.04 are inside. Body is 7.79 dB, outside: about 10 dB fuller in the low band than the real median. Real vocals are usually low-cut in the mix; ours was not.
- **Change, tuned after the fact on one sheet:** a low shelf cut on the voice under about 250 Hz. At 12 dB, body reads 1.83 dB on the clean voice (inside, near the top). The cut lowers the voice 1.5 dB, so each style's beat gain came down by the same amount. The owner listened and made it the engine's default. Six styles re-rendered: voice minus beat -3.58 to -3.77 dB, track gate 0 of 11 outside in all, all nine breath measures inside (median unbroken run 0.88 s, down from 1.04 s, nearer the bottom of the real range).
- **Beat lift at repeated lines (same day, tuning, no declared test).** On that default the backing read 1 to 2 dB quieter at repeated lines than at other lines (drums -1.99, bass -1.09, other -0.90 dB; real medians 0.13, 0.23, 1.33). The engine now raises the beat 2.5 dB while a repeated line is sung, with short ramps; each beat gain came down 0.75 dB with it. After: drums -0.46, bass 0.68, other 1.28 dB; voice minus beat -3.59 to -3.86 dB over six styles; gate and breath unchanged. The owner listened and made it the default. "Repeated line" is not "hook".
- **Deliberate wordplay on a word with two pronunciations.** A marker in the lyric sheet (`word{say}`) says one use of a word another way. The voice, the syllable counts and the rhyme measures follow the sound, and other uses of the word are still flagged by the lint. Test written first; not yet heard in a render.
- **Limits:** one planted change, so only brightness is change-checked (body and flatness are level-checked); band levels of a separated stem carry the separator's colour and leaked cymbals in the air band; microphone, mix chain and performer are not told apart; reads stop at 11 kHz; hip hop only; one lyric sheet and one voice pair of ours; the low cut and the lift were tuned on that sheet, and after the low cut the tone was re-read on the clean voice only. Not started: rhyme position in the bar (needs downbeats and word times good to a beat subdivision, neither validated) and the pop/rock side of interludes.
